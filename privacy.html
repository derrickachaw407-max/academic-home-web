Academic-Home

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 2026
Definitions
In this document, these words have the meanings below.

account noun — your registered place in Academic-Home, identified by your email address and protected by your password.

content noun — the files, text, notes, slides and other material you put into Academic-Home or create with it.

personal data noun — any information that identifies you, or could be used to identify you, such as your name or email address.

processing noun — anything done with data — collecting, recording, storing, using, sharing or deleting it.

memory noun — in Academic-Home, the short notes RamzAI keeps about your studies, which you can see, change, delete or switch off.

AI abbreviation — artificial intelligence: computer systems that produce answers or text in a way that resembles human reasoning — in Academic-Home, RamzAI and the writing tools.

we, us, our pronouns — Academic-Home and the people who run it. you, your: the person using Academic-Home.

About this policy
This policy explains, in plain words, what Academic-Home (formerly Docente) collects, why, where it goes, how long it's kept and what you can do about it. It covers the Academic-Home app on the web and in the App Store and Google Play. We've tried to describe exactly what the app does — not more, not less. If anything here is unclear, email us at support.docente@gmail.com.
What we keep on our server
Your name and email address; your password in scrambled (hashed) form — never the password itself; your plan (which Scholar pass, if any, and when it ends), the AI points it came with and how many are left, points you bought on their own, how many points were used today, how many of a pass’s research points are left, and how many of this month’s free points and research writings were used; a history of your conversions (the kinds of file and the dates — not the file names) so you can see Your activity; notes RamzAI and the writing assistant pick up about your studies (like your course, a project or a citation style) — memory is on until you switch it off; RamzAI tells you under its answer each time it notes something, and you can see, edit, delete or switch off the notes in Profile → RamzAI’s memory. The notes go with your messages to Anthropic, our AI provider, so RamzAI can use them. RamzAI is told never to note health, money, religion or relationships; if one appears, delete it; referral codes you make and when; and records of store purchases (described below).
What we never collect
We don't collect your contacts, your location, or anything from other apps. Academic-Home uses your camera and microphone only when you choose to: to take a photo you attach or a page you scan, to type by voice or to have your talk heard while you rehearse it (what you say goes to your browser’s speech service — Google’s in Chrome, Apple’s in Safari — which turns it into text; Academic-Home only gets the text when you send it, and a rehearsal keeps only how many words you said and how often “um” came up, never the words), or in a meeting you join — where your voice and picture go live to the people in it and are never recorded (see Meetings). Academic-Home has no advertising, no advertising trackers and no analytics companies watching what you do. We never see or store your card or mobile money details.
Your files
Files you convert, make smaller, preview, open or save in the PDF or Word editor, protect with a password or unlock, present in a meeting (Word and PowerPoint), open in Slide Studio, or read into the AI tools are processed in a temporary, isolated place purely to do what you asked, and deleted from our server as soon as the job is done — usually within seconds. We don't keep copies, we don't look at them, and we don't use them to train any AI. The finished file is sent back to you, and a copy is kept on your own device, in Your activity, so you can download it again. Pages you scan are found, straightened and cleaned on your own device, not on our server; they wait there until you start a new scan or log out, and reach us only if you ask RamzAI to read them. Your library (papers you save from Research, and PDFs you add) is kept on your own device until you log out; it reaches us only with a research you ask to use it in, and isn't kept on our server. Pictures you convert into another kind of picture or a PDF, and a single Markdown, text, web page or CSV file you convert into another, are converted on your own device; if that fails, the app asks before sending the file to our server. A batch of several such files is converted on our server and deleted straight after. A template or guide you give for research writing is read once on our server to find its headings and instructions, and deleted there straight after; what was found is kept on your own device with the rest of what you entered.
AI features and who sees your text
When you use RamzAI in any of its three ways (Chat, Research, with any saved papers and PDFs from your library that you choose to use, or Study), ask it for slides, a summary, study notes or a quiz, use automatic or manual research writing, or use RamzAI in the Word editor or in Slide Studio, the text (and any file text) you send is passed to our AI provider, Anthropic, to produce the answer. For defence slides the whole of the research work you give is sent (up to about 60 pages), so that every choice in it can be found; it is not kept on our server. See Anthropic's privacy policy for how it handles that data. To find real research papers for RamzAI’s Research, research writing and slides on a topic, the topic of your request — not your account details — is also sent to the open research databases OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and Europe PMC; when an answer needs something current, the topic goes to a web search service. In Research, our server then fetches each paper it reads from Europe PMC or from the site that holds the paper’s free copy, reads it and deletes the file at once: that site sees a request from our server and nothing about you. Before a research or a research writing starts, the app asks who it is for and what you need. What you enter there — who it is for (for example a student and the level, a lecturer, an organisation), your field, the place, the topic, the details you choose (among them the citation style and any example reference you give, the kinds of sources, the language and how the Word file should look), the headings and instructions of your template, your answers to RamzAI’s questions, and for a research proposal the plan of your study (where it will be done, who will take part and about how many, your objectives, how the study will be done, and how many researchers there are) — is sent to Anthropic, so that RamzAI can help you choose a topic, ask about anything unclear and do the work to fit you. When the first part of a research proposal is written and RamzAI’s memory is on, the proposal’s title is added to RamzAI’s memory, which is kept on our server; you are shown the note, and can change it, delete it or switch the memory off. The rest is kept on your own device, not on our server: a research you haven’t confirmed and the details of a piece you are writing for up to 60 days, and who it is for until you log out, so that the next one starts there. A name and an institution you give for a title page are kept on your own device and sent to our server only when you download the Word file, to be printed on it; they are not kept there and are not sent to Anthropic. What you add for a research proposal’s cover page and consent form (the researchers’ names and index numbers, your institution and programme, your supervisor’s name, a contact, and a logo if you choose one) is treated the same way: the logo is kept on your own device until you remove it or log out. The start and length of your work plan and the costs of your budget go to our server with the proposal, to make those two appendices; they are not sent to Anthropic.
Meetings
When you host or join a meeting, your voice, your video, a screen you share, the chat, reactions and any file someone presents travel live between the people in the meeting through LiveKit, the service that carries Academic-Home's meetings. Academic-Home never records or keeps them: chat and presented files are gone when the meeting ends, and Academic-Home never asks LiveKit to record a meeting (LiveKit may keep technical records of the connections to run its service — see LiveKit's privacy policy). On our server we keep the meeting's title, code and settings (and, when the host schedules it, its time, time zone and agenda), and its attendance list — the name each person joined with, whether they used an Academic-Home account, when they joined and left, and how long they stayed — so the host and co-hosts can download it. In an end-to-end encrypted meeting, the key is part of the invite link (after the #) and is never sent to our server: neither we nor LiveKit can see or hear the meeting or read its chat, and RamzAI’s notes are switched off. A Word or PowerPoint file the host presents is first turned into pages on our server and deleted straight after; a PDF never leaves the host’s device. Every other meeting is encrypted on its way between your device and LiveKit. If the host turns on live captions, each person decides whether their own voice is captioned: if they say yes, their browser's speech service turns what they say into words, which go to the people in the meeting like chat (locked with the meeting's key in an encrypted meeting) and are gone when the meeting ends. If the host asks RamzAI for notes, the meeting's chat, the captions the host's device showed and the first words of the pages shown are sent to Anthropic to write them; the notes stay on the host's device unless the host emails them to the people who came with an Academic-Home account. If the host asks RamzAI for an agenda, the meeting's name, how long it is and anything the host typed for it are sent to Anthropic to write it. If the host emails invitations, we send the meeting's name, time, link and agenda to the addresses the host typed, and keep only how many invitations they sent that day (to stop misuse), never the addresses.
Sounds and the focus timer
The sounds in Study are made on your device and play from it; nothing is sent anywhere, and the app no longer plays songs from YouTube. Your focus-timer record stays on your device.
Free points and your device
Free AI points (5 every month) are given once per device, not once per account. To make that work, the app makes a random identifier the first time it runs on a device, keeps it on that device, and sends it with your sign-in. Our server keeps the identifier next to the accounts that used it. It says nothing about you or your device beyond "this device already has its free points"; it is not shared with anyone, and it is kept after an account is deleted, so a phone can’t get free points twice.
Error reports and technical logs
If the app hits an unexpected error on your phone, it sends a short technical report: the error message, where in the app it happened, the app version and your browser type. It contains no name, email, files or chat content. Like almost every online service, our server’s logs also record technical details of requests (never what you type or your files), including IP addresses, which we use to stop abuse (for example, limiting how many requests one connection can make).
Moving to the new address
Academic-Home's address is now acadhome.vercel.app. A phone keeps what a web app saves apart for each address, so when you open an old address (such as getdocente.vercel.app), Academic-Home brings what your phone saved there (your decks, quizzes, chats, files, settings and sign-in) to the new one. A big move asks you first and says how much data it uses. Before anything leaves your phone, it's locked (encrypted) there with a new key that only the moving link carries, after the # (the part of a link browsers never send to a server). Our server holds the locked parts for at most an hour, can't open them, and deletes them as soon as they arrive. At the new address, the app adds only what isn't there yet. Nothing moves unless you open an old address.
Kept only on your phone
Your chats with RamzAI and automatic research writing (the one open and those kept in Chats), your practice quizzes, scores and review schedule (which questions come back when), your exam dates and the days you studied, your focus timer and today's study time, your Slide Studio decks and their pictures (and a copy of any PowerPoint you opened there, so the one you download keeps its design), the times from rehearsing a deck, the files you made (in Your activity, up to 40 at a time), your research reports (the newest 60), the summaries RamzAI made from your notes and your defence slides (the newest 60, with the notes a summary was made from, but not the research work a defence was made from), the PDF you were last editing, the Word documents you worked on in Write (the newest 8, with a file you opened to change), the signatures you saved, your notifications, your study music settings and the mixes you saved, your theme, Easier reading, Data saver and vibration settings, the way RamzAI last worked for you (Chat, Research or Study), how much data the app used on the phone this month and last (counted there and never sent to us), the name you last joined a meeting with, the keys of encrypted meetings you made, the times of your next meetings (for Home and the reminder), what you're typing in a box and haven't sent yet (for up to 7 days, so a dropped connection or a closed app doesn't lose it; never passwords or codes), the name, email address and plan of the account you're signed in with (so the app opens without waiting for our server), and a copy of the app itself and its fonts (so it opens without a signal) are saved on your phone, not on our server. Logging out removes your chats, research reports, summaries and defence slides, your library, quizzes, exam dates, decks, files, notifications, what you hadn't sent yet, the PDF you were last editing and your Word documents, saved signatures, rehearsal times, and your meeting name, keys and times from the phone; your settings and the app itself stay. Clearing your browser’s site data removes all of it. If you allow alerts, your browser shows them on your device when a file is ready or a focus round ends; nothing about them is sent to us. Read aloud uses your phone’s own voices, so what it reads stays on your phone, unless you pick a voice marked online: that sends the words being read to your browser’s voice service (Google’s, for example) to turn them into speech.
Purchases
Paid plans are bought through the App Store or Google Play, which handle payment. On the web, payments are handled by Paystack — we receive the payment reference, amount and outcome, never your card or mobile money details. We receive and keep the store's transaction ID (or Paystack's reference) and the product you bought, to credit your purchase and to act on renewals, cancellations and refunds the stores tell us about. Refunds themselves are handled by Apple or Google.
Referral codes
When you make a referral code we store the code, who made it and when it expires. When someone joins with your code, we record that (which account joined with whose code, and when) so that you get your points.
Who we share data with
Only the services Academic-Home needs to work: our hosting providers (Render runs our server and database; Vercel serves the app), LiveKit (meetings), an email service (sign-in codes, and the meeting invitations and notes you choose to send), Anthropic (AI answers), OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC and a web search service (finding research papers and sources), Paystack (payments), Apple and Google (older store purchases, and Google sign-in: its button loads only on the sign-in screen), and Cloudflare (it delivers the code that shows PDFs). Paystack receives your email address and your Academic-Home account number with a payment. These services may keep data outside Ghana. We don't sell your data, rent it, or share it for advertising. We'd only disclose data if the law genuinely required it.
How long we keep things
Your account and what's stored with it, until you delete your account. Your conversion history, until you clear it or delete your account. Files, only for the seconds needed to process them (the copy in Your activity stays on your device, not with us). A record of each payment (reference, plan, amount and date), because tax and accounting law require payment records: after you delete your account it is kept without your name or email. A meeting and its attendance list, until 90 days after the meeting (we delete them automatically). The locked parts of a move to the new address, for at most an hour. Server logs and error reports are kept for a limited time by our hosting provider and then removed automatically. If your connection drops while the server is answering a request (an answer from RamzAI, a meeting, a payment, a conversion), the answer is kept for up to an hour so the app can still collect it without doing the work, or taking points, twice; then it is deleted.
Keeping it safe
Passwords are stored hashed, everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS), and requests are limited to stop abuse. In a meeting, nobody can change their own name once they're in, people without an Academic-Home account are marked as guests, and only the host and co-hosts decide who comes in. No online service can promise perfect security, but we take care to collect as little as possible — the safest data is data we never hold.
Young users
Academic-Home is made for students and tutors. If you're under the age at which you can agree to this kind of data use in your country, please use Academic-Home with a parent's or guardian's permission.
Your rights and choices
You can see your details in Profile, clear your activity any time, see, edit or delete what RamzAI remembers — or switch its memory off — in RamzAI's memory, turn vibration off, log out, or delete your whole account from Profile → Delete account — which removes your account, its history and RamzAI’s memory at once. We keep, without your name or email, a record of each payment (accounting rules require it), and your phone’s random mark, so it can’t get free points twice. Your name stays in the attendance list of a meeting someone else hosted until that list is deleted, 90 days after the meeting. You can also email us to ask what we hold about you or to correct it. We aim to handle your data in line with Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), and to honour these rights wherever you live.
Changes to this policy
If we change how Academic-Home handles data, we'll update this policy and its date first. Big changes will be explained in the app before they apply.
